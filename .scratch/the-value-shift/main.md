# Scratch — Episode 1: The Value Shift

**Position in arc:** 1 of 11 (the opener — sets personal stakes, names what the industry sees, points past it).
**Binding question:** Why does AI feel like a threat to senior engineers — and what's the durable thing it can't take?
**Target length:** 12–18 minutes (≈2200 spoken words at 150 wpm).
**Date:** 2026-05-20

---

## Episode's job in the series arc

Open the series with the personal stake (Michael as a real engineer feeling real pressure, not as a guru), name the value shift the industry has named, then point past the industry framing toward what the gospel sees that the industry doesn't (judgment as eternal intelligence, agency as the irreplaceable thing). Sets up Episode 2 (the four disciplines and their limits) and the whole arc toward Episode 6 (the seven unmapped steps).

---

## Outline (5-part structure per `episodes/README.md`)

1. **The engineering problem** (~2:30) — The senior engineer crisis: the thing that made me valuable for 18 years isn't scarce anymore. Lead with the personal feeling: "I feel insignificant sometimes."
2. **What the industry says** (~2:30) — The value shift framework. Multiple industry voices saying judgment > execution. The industry's contribution and its limits.
3. **What actually happened** (~4:00) — Sitting down to write the Staying Relevant study in March 2026. The study itself was the evidence. Include the failure side: prompt spirals, days of yelling at the model.
4. **The pattern underneath** (~4:00) — D&C 130:18-19. The intelligence is the judgment. AI has no resurrection. Abraham 4–5 organizing-watching pattern. Parable of the Talents — "am I using what I've been given?"
5. **What this means for you** (~1:30) — Stop measuring by execution speed. Take inventory. Treat AI as partner you direct. Invest in the durable layer.

## Ben Test pre-check — claim calibration

Every claim about practice needs calibrated language per the Ben Test skill: **practiced** (consistent, evidence in the work), **occasional** (real but not systematic), **aspirational** (we want to but don't yet), **mythical** (we say it but don't actually do it).

| Claim | Calibration | How it lands in the script |
|---|---|---|
| "I direct AI's work" | Practiced | Keep — the project state is the evidence. |
| "I catch the 10% AI gets wrong" | Practiced (with honest misses) | Keep, but include the failure beat so it doesn't sound infallible. |
| "I built MCP servers that actually work" | Practiced | Keep — six servers in `scripts/`, hosted at `engine.ibeco.me`. |
| "The partnership produced what neither could alone" | Practiced (this project is the evidence) | Keep — back with the specific moment (writing the Staying Relevant study). |
| "I sometimes spend hours yelling at the model" | Practiced | Include — this is the failure that earns the rest. |
| "I'm more valuable than the previous version of me" | Honest claim, framed carefully | Phrase as "the version of me adapting to the shift" — not bragging. |
| "Investing in judgment will keep you safe" | Aspirational at scale, practiced for me | Frame as "this is what's worked for me" not "this is the answer." |

**Things to NOT claim:**
- That we've mastered the partnership. We haven't — we're discovering.
- That this approach is generalizable. It's our specific approach with evidence in our specific work.
- That the 11-step cycle is fully practiced. It's partially practiced; we're working on it.

## Voice — what to keep out (per `study/yt/voice-analysis-ai-vs-michael.md`)

Cut list — these are presenter tics that make the script sound like a YouTube hustle video instead of like Michael:

- "Let that land"
- "Sit with that"
- "Here's the thing"
- "This matters because"
- "Read that again"
- "That's not nothing"
- "stops me cold"

**Em-dash budget:** one per spoken paragraph max. (Citation dashes don't count.)

**Use therefore/but causation**, not "and then" sequence. Every beat earns the next.

**Don't narrate the script's own structure.** Don't say "in this episode I'm going to" or "the next thing I want to tell you" — just say the thing.

## Failure to include (per teaching covenant — `failures-included`)

The script must include moments where the AI was wrong, where the partnership didn't work, where Michael felt small. The personal vulnerability ("I feel insignificant sometimes") is the keystone. The prompt-spiral failure is the counter-evidence that earns the rest of the argument. The "I haven't written a single line of code in four months" anecdote from another engineer is honest data — others feel this too.

If the script reads as triumphant without these, it's covenant-broken.
